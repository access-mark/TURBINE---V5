01 / 12

Customers, contacts and CRM

Establish the customer record as the commercial source of truth.

What this covers

Organisations, people, locations, roles, communication preferences, relationship owners and account history belong together. A new enquiry should not require recreating a customer in another department.

Who uses it and why

Sole proprietor: remember every client commitment without a spreadsheet. IT services: see commercial, support and renewal context. Professional firm: distinguish the client organisation, decision-maker and individual engagements. Distributor: coordinate account contacts, delivery sites and purchasing history.

Governance and management visibility

Duplicate records, ownership changes, stale contact details, visibility boundaries and permission to access sensitive customer information all require controls. Managers need pipeline movement, overdue follow-ups and the history behind a customer balance.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Capture an enquiry; establish whether the prospect already exists; qualify the need; assign an owner; record conversations and next actions; create a quotation; retain the accepted scope; link delivery and subsequent billing to the same customer context.

02 / 12

Sales, quotations and acceptance

Turn commercial intent into an approved, traceable obligation.

What this covers

Opportunities should carry the customer need, responsible salesperson, expected value, scope assumptions and next decision. Quotations should identify products or services, quantities, prices, applicable tax treatment, validity, exclusions and the version sent.

Who uses it and why

Consultant: quote a fixed-fee assignment. Contractor: quote labour, materials and milestones. Reseller: quote stock-dependent equipment. Agency: quote a recurring retainer and separate campaign deliverables.

Governance and management visibility

Price overrides, discount authority, expired offers, changed scope and acceptance evidence must be explicit. Reports should distinguish quoted, accepted, lost and unfulfilled business.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Qualify demand; draft scope and commercial terms; request internal approval when required; issue a versioned quotation; record customer acceptance; preserve the accepted version; initiate the resulting work or fulfilment and retain the commercial audit trail.

03 / 12

Work, projects and scheduling

Translate promises into owned and measurable delivery.

What this covers

Operational work needs tasks, milestones, dependencies, assignees, due dates, customer commitments, supporting records and exception handling. A project is not merely a task list: it carries agreed outcomes and the resources needed to achieve them.

Who uses it and why

Solo consultant: schedule engagements and deliverables. Agency: manage campaigns and approval cycles. IT service firm: coordinate implementation and support jobs. Contractor: manage site work and subcontractor dependencies.

Governance and management visibility

Scope changes, late milestones, incomplete handovers and unapproved work must be visible. Management needs work-in-progress, capacity, overdue items and the connection between delivery and revenue.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Create delivery work from an accepted commitment; assign ownership; plan milestones and appointments; record progress, materials or time where relevant; surface blockers; confirm completion; hand off billable outcomes to finance.

04 / 12

Procurement and supplier operations

Control the full purchasing obligation, not just the purchase order.

What this covers

Supplier records, purchasing requests, approval thresholds, orders, delivery evidence, invoices and outstanding commitments need a coherent chain. Demand can originate in projects, stock replenishment or normal operating expenditure.

Who uses it and why

Manufacturer: buy production inputs. IT reseller: procure customer equipment. Practice administrator: purchase consumables. Construction business: control materials by project.

Governance and management visibility

Segregation of duties, duplicate supplier invoices, partial deliveries, supplier disputes and budget exceptions require traceability. Report committed versus actual spend and outstanding receipts.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Identify need; check budget and authority; request or compare supplier prices; approve; issue purchase order; record goods or services received; match supplier invoice; resolve differences; pass approved liability to finance.

05 / 12

Inventory, stock and cost

Know what is available, where it is and what it costs.

What this covers

Products, units of measure, stock locations, receipts, issues, adjustments, reservations and cost relationships should connect to purchasing and fulfilment. Inventory is not universal: service-only businesses may need little or no stock handling.

Who uses it and why

Retailer: avoid selling unavailable goods. Distributor: coordinate warehouse and customer delivery. IT provider: allocate devices and parts to customer work. Manufacturer: track inputs and finished items.

Governance and management visibility

Negative stock, unauthorised adjustments, damaged goods, inconsistent valuation and incomplete counts need explicit rules. Useful views include availability, slow-moving stock, replenishment needs and margin.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Define catalogue and locations; receive purchased items; validate quantities; reserve stock against demand; issue or fulfil; record returns or adjustments; reconcile physical counts and costs; reflect the financial impact.

06 / 12

Finance, billing and receivables

Make money movements traceable to the business activity behind them.

What this covers

Customer billing, credit notes, receivables, supplier obligations, expenses, tax treatment, transaction history and period controls should form a consistent financial record. Financial correctness requires stronger qualification than attractive dashboards.

Who uses it and why

Freelancer: track issued invoices and late payers. Agency: manage recurring retainers and project extras. Distributor: connect delivery to invoicing. Services firm: assess billable work and collections.

Governance and management visibility

No claim of statutory compliance or accounting readiness is made without verification. Reversals, adjustments, audit history, deferred revenue, VAT, reconciliations and segregation of duties are qualification requirements.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Generate an invoice from an accepted or completed billable obligation; validate amounts and tax; issue and record it; allocate receipts; manage disputes or credits; reconcile balances; close periods under appropriate controls.

07 / 12

Banking interfaces and financial controls

Use external financial rails without turning TURBINE into an integration marketplace.

What this covers

Bank accounts, imported statements, payment references, allocation rules and reconciliation evidence should support the primary TURBINE financial record. Banks and payment networks are external infrastructure, not replacement business applications.

Who uses it and why

Sole proprietor: see what customers have paid. Distributor: match high-volume receipts. Professional firm: manage retainers and disbursements. Multi-team SME: control who can approve or amend allocations.

Governance and management visibility

Consent, bank-data security, reconciliation completeness, duplicate imports and payment approval authority must be independently verified. Specific banking connectivity is not asserted as live.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Receive authorised transaction information; match receipts and payments to open items; investigate unmatched entries; approve corrections; retain evidence; reconcile to the relevant ledger and reporting period.

08 / 12

Reporting and management visibility

Move from isolated numbers to an explainable operating picture.

What this covers

A useful management view joins commercial demand, work commitments, purchasing, stock where relevant, cash collection and financial outcomes. Every figure should have a clear definition, reporting period and source record.

Who uses it and why

Owner-operator: what needs attention today? Sales manager: what is likely to close? Operations lead: what is late? Finance lead: what is owed and why?

Governance and management visibility

Dashboards must not conceal missing or unqualified data. Role-based access, calculation definitions, freshness, exports and auditability are part of the reporting design.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Choose a business question; identify authoritative records; calculate an agreed measure; highlight variance or exceptions; drill into the underlying transactions; assign an action and monitor whether it resolves the issue.

Turbine Guide and contextual intelligence

Make a complete operating system navigable for everyday users.

What this covers

Turbine Guide is the intended contextual assistance layer: task-oriented navigation, explanation of business terms, step-by-step journeys and guidance informed by the current workflow and user permissions.

Who uses it and why

First-time founder: learn how to issue a quotation. New employee: understand a purchasing approval. Manager: find overdue commitments. Finance clerk: follow an exception-resolution journey.

Governance and management visibility

AI-assisted suggestions must remain explainable, permission-aware and distinguish suggestions from committed transactions. Automated or generative functions require separate security and accuracy qualification.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

A user states an objective; the Guide identifies the relevant workflow; presents the required information and next steps; routes to the appropriate screen; explains validation errors; and keeps the person responsible for authorised actions.

DataPort Import / Export Studio

Make full adoption practical without permanent dependency on old software.

What this covers

DataPort is the intended governed migration and portability studio: file templates, mapping, transformation, validation, preview, bulk import, error handling, retry and export. Its purpose is a controlled move into TURBINE, not indefinite synchronisation of disconnected business apps.

Who uses it and why

Consultancy: migrate clients, contacts and invoices. Distributor: migrate product and supplier records. Growing SME: migrate open opportunities, work and financial opening balances.

Governance and management visibility

Thousands of records may require batch processing, resumability and rollback strategy. Accounting and bank migration require reconciliation. Actual throughput and supported file types must be qualified before promises are made.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Inventory source datasets; choose the destination record model; map fields and relationships; test with a sample; validate duplicates and required values; reconcile totals; approve cutover; import in controlled batches; retain exception reports and export rights.

12 / 12

Access, permissions and audit

Protect organisational boundaries and accountable decision-making.

What this covers

Identity, user lifecycle, roles, tenant isolation, delegated authority, sensitive-record permissions and immutable-enough audit evidence are foundational controls. These are prerequisites for trusted operation, not optional extras.

Who uses it and why

Sole proprietor: retain simple owner control. Small team: separate sales and finance responsibilities. Established SME: enforce approval thresholds and department access.

Governance and management visibility

Tenant isolation, authentication, audit/outbox integrity, recovery and restoration remain subject to executable qualification gates. Do not infer production security from the existence of a UI.

How it fits the whole BusinessOS

This is not a standalone module to buy or integrate. Its records and decisions participate in the complete TURBINE operating lifecycle, subject to qualified availability and the organisation’s adopted configuration.

Operating journey

Invite a user under approved authority; assign least-privilege access; verify identity; permit only authorised actions; record significant changes; review access periodically; revoke access when responsibilities change.
